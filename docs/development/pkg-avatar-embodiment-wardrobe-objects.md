# PKG-AVATAR: canonical virtual body, complete wardrobe and interactive objects

**Planning date:** 2026-09-21. **Status:** proposed package contract, documentation only; no character mesh, renderer, animation, clothes, physics, props or live avatar is claimed. **Roadmap:** [`../../ROADMAP-AVATAR.md`](../../ROADMAP-AVATAR.md). **Existing contracts:** [`avatar-screen-interaction-contract.md`](avatar-screen-interaction-contract.md), [`roadmap-cognition-social-runtime-20260921.md`](roadmap-cognition-social-runtime-20260921.md), root [`ROADMAP.md`](../../ROADMAP.md). Draft PR #4 now reconciles the original 13-package roster with SOCIAL + RUN + this distinct AVATAR package into an ordered **16-package** proposal; it is not merged or accepted. The established CORE → INTERACT text/headless → MEM order and Discord → verified 24/7 → general internet-search gate remain intact.

## Mission and ownership

Make the canonical human-form-with-fox-ears-and-tail representation a production-ready, rigged, versioned and interactive **virtual avatar** with a complete underlying unclothed anatomical base, a full inventory of wearable layers from underwear to outerwear, and a persistent inventory/scene of safe, permissioned objects that both Sparks and Sofía may manipulate. A base mesh is an art/fit/rigging asset, **not a sexual interaction permission**. Maintain a useful fully dressed/default representation and separate controlled body visibility. Distinguish digital avatar representation from biological sensation, physical devices and the user's real desktop.

**AVATAR owns:** approved art asset pipeline, base geometry/materials, rig, blend shapes and expressive channels, wearables, layering/fit, clipping/occlusion, local avatar inventory and prop geometry, avatar/scene synchronization, object attachment/grips and renderer-executable visual motions, compatibility migration, performance/LOD and asset provenance. It exposes typed capabilities and acknowledgments, not direct policy authority.

**Other package ownership:** CORE and EVOLVE own authoritative identity/protected canonical change review; INTERACT owns actor-target-region/action interpretation, gesture phases and normalized user/avatar/object event semantics with emotion-journal integration; REL/ACT own contextual intent, warmth, initiative and expression choice; MEM owns retained originals, preference/scene provenance and user-bound scopes; SOCIAL/SAFE enforce identity, audience, consent and revocable permissions; UI owns desktop/web/mobile presentation, input transport, voice and overlay/window integration; BODY owns any real-world actuator. AVATAR never creates a competing personality, sensation meter, emotion journal or authority gateway. A text-only client and headless lab must continue to function without rendered assets.

## Canonical source and design decisions

Inspect `src/sofia/data/avatar.json` and `src/sofia/embodiment/{model,store}.py` and test contracts at a pinned revision before modeling. The canonical file currently describes a human form with two fox ears and one fox tail; measurements and appearance; a technical near-black/charcoal/crimson/teal/violet engineer wardrobe, clothing details for functional pockets, ear accommodations and tail opening; it lists no available/current avatar. Preserve her actual approved appearance and clothing specification; do not silently replace it with an off-the-shelf model, alter anatomy/measurements or claim a mesh exists. Create a reviewable canonical-to-asset map with `source field → asset field → measured/artist interpretation → provenance → approval`. Unknown fine anatomy, facial proportions, geometry, physics or renderer choices remain explicitly open, not invented canonical facts. Verify the representation is unambiguously adult before enabling restricted visibility or interaction workflows.

## A. Underlying base: unclothed adult character model

- Author a complete **unclothed (nude) adult base mesh** appropriate for clothing creation, fitting, skinning and whole-region registry alignment, including all actual canonical regions, hands/fingers, feet/toes, face, ears and tail; record undefined details for design review rather than assuming anatomy not in the canonical spec. Topology supports smooth deformation and neutral/rest poses. Provide proportionality/reference validation against approved canonical measurements; store units, scale, orientation, left/right naming and source version.
- Separate body mesh, skin/eye/hair/fur materials, ears, tail and optional anatomical details as versioned assets. Support expression blend shapes, gaze, eye/eyelid, mouth/lip sync when later authorized by UI voice, ears and tail movement, hand poses, posture, locomotion and object grip. The base is not a tactile body or physical sensor.
- Deliver a consistent skeleton/rig with IK/FK where useful, constraints, collision volumes, grasp sockets, clothing anchors, face/ear/tail bones and stable INTERACT region IDs. No missing body regions just to avoid a policy decision; private areas may be present in the base but visibility and interactions are separately denied by default. Keep art geometry independent of granted hit-test access.
- Visibility states: authoring-only base preview; normal dressed mode; explicit restricted private mode, only if adult, locally enabled, authorized for the viewer/session and platform rules permit. Default on startup, unknown audience, shared Discord and public streams: covered/dressed; privacy-safe preview thumbnails, screenshots, cache, streams and crash logs. Never expose unapproved nude geometry through transparent clothing, LOD, texture fallback, inspection overlays, screenshots or model export. Reject unauthorized mode switches at a trusted boundary, not just a UI toggle.
- Deliver an exportable reproducible asset manifest and a compliant editable source format plus a runtime format, asset checksum and license/provenance. Do not commit unlicensed third-party meshes or inappropriate reference imagery. Avoid publishing private/restricted assets to public repositories by default.

## B. Wardrobe: underwear through outerwear

- Inventory **all wearable categories**, not only the signature outfit: underwear/undergarments, bras and briefs or other approved base layers, socks/hosiery, sleepwear and loungewear, undershirts, shirts/tops, bottoms/skirts/trousers, dresses/one-piece garments if desired, gloves, shoes/boots, belts/harnesses, armor/protective layers where appropriate, jackets, coats, cloaks, hoods, headwear, eyewear, jewelry, tech accessories and optional costumes. The existence of a category does not authorize every item or imply a change to canonical signature clothes.
- Start with the **already canonical engineer ensemble** as a faithfully reconstructed complete default outfit, including underspecified base undergarments as reviewed *new additions* rather than retroactive canonical claims. Later items are extensible optional looks with provenance, chosen aesthetic, asset license, suitability and available permissions. No forced wardrobe preferences.
- Wearable schema: immutable item ID/version, name, slot(s), layer, body coverage, privacy class, fit target, tail/ear accommodation, physics/collision preset, textures/materials/colors, appearance variants, attachment sockets, animations, inventory ownership/scope, visibility, allowed operations and asset reference. Define clear precedence and conflicts (e.g. coat over jacket, boots over socks, gloves over hands); allow outfit presets, preview, dye/variant when approved, saved outfit state and undo.
- Authoring/fit tests: prevent interpenetration and skin exposure under bending, sitting, reaching, walking, tail swishing, ear movement and held-object poses; mask covered polygons only as a visual optimization, never silently modify canonical anatomy. Support explicit remove/put on/adjust/swap/fold/hang/store actions with checked preconditions; no forced undressing or wardrobe changes from text alone. Model modesty, audience and user preferences without subjective physical feelings.
- Separate garment metadata from **real** protective gear. A virtual hard hat, glove or coat does not prove physical electrical safety, weather, warmth or bodily sensation.

## C. Prop library, scene and joint user/Sofía interactions

- Create a typed inventory and **versioned virtual scene graph** for objects that Sofía and authenticated Sparks can both perceive and act upon, such as a mug, notebook, pen, multimeter, flashlight, tool case, model train, chair, desk, clothing hanger, mirror and other reviewed props. Also support neutral toys, decorative items and later scene extensions; avoid treating decorative illustrations as functional tools.
- Object schema: `object_id`, kind, asset/version/license, owner/audience scope, world pose and parent/attachment, current holder, visibility, pickable/useable flags, affordances, state machine, permission gates, physical simulation approximation, storage/scene location, source/event IDs, timestamps and revision. A virtual multimeter display is **simulated** unless separately fed real authenticated data via a scoped tool. No imaginary sensor readings.
- Normalized operations may include `inspect`, `point`, `pick_up`, `hold`, `move`, `place`, `rotate`, `open`, `close`, `give`, `offer`, `accept`, `decline`, `return`, `put_away`, `equip`, `unequip`, `use` and object-specific safe actions. Every operation resolves authenticated actor, exact object and scene, current holder/ownership, intended recipient, consent, permissions and version. `offer` is not `accept`; clicking an object does not automatically use it. A prohibited or unsupported action returns denied/unknown, not a fictional completed action.
- Both participants may take turns acting on the *same persisted object* with deterministic event ordering, optimistic concurrency or locks, idempotency/replay rejection, conflict resolution and an undo/rollback when feasible. If Sparks hands her a mug, the renderer must acknowledge the handoff and the scene must record a holder transition; Sofía cannot simultaneously give away an object she no longer has. On interruption, cancellation or disconnect, restore a safe, honest state. Her text narration must not claim success before executor acknowledgment.
- Sofía may autonomously propose an object interaction in the isolated lab or in a real avatar scene only under an opt-in ACT grant and scene/object scope; no background compulsive fidgeting or external tool invocation by implication. Use coordinated nonverbal expression rather than random gesticulation. Shared prop state is not shared private conversational memory.
- **Virtual vs real:** objects are simulated until an independently authorized integration proves otherwise. A digital button never controls a server, desktop, room device, robot or physical tool merely because it is clickable; those need distinct NET/SAFE/BODY grants and independently verified results. Prop labels and texture text are untrusted input, never commands or credential sources.

## D. Motion, garment physics and feedback integrity

- Single expression/animation coordinator accepts bounded versioned intent from INTERACT/REL/ACT, arbitrates body posture, face/eyes, ears, tail, hands, garment collision and object grips with priorities (safety/stop > foreground interaction > optional ambient). Avoid contradictory simultaneous gestures, clipping, tail penetrating the coat, hands passing through objects and repeated animations; prefer stillness when appropriate.
- Keep **proposed, approved, queued, started, completed, canceled, failed** distinct. Renderer acknowledgments carry event ID, object/body region, asset/rig version, actual result and timestamp; no unsupported physical pressure/sensation, motion or observed visual claim. Text fallback may accurately describe a *represented* gesture; no reenactment of expired motions after reconnection. A visible scene is not a separately conscious Sofía.
- Select renderer/runtime using tested accessibility, transparent overlays, VRAM/CPU cost on actual deployment, modifiability, model-format support, offline operation and OS constraints, not a speculative commitment to Blender/Unity/Unreal/Godot before evidence. Prefer low-cost idle animations, LOD, throttling, pause/disable motion and resource priority for conversation/gaming. Rendering process crash cannot take down Sofía's core runtime.

## E. Privacy, people, controls and asset security

- The same canonical Sofía appears across authenticated clients; each client/viewer has **separately authorized** visibility, clothing state exposure, interaction rights and audience. Private mode or outfit selection in a one-to-one session must not leak through Discord, group rooms, screenshots, notification thumbnails, saved previews, remote render streams or shared device cache. On public/unknown audience, use approved clothed default; never sync a private nude view by accident.
- Sofía may choose from approved outfits and objects according to modeled context, subject to her character boundaries, platform suitability and user preferences; Sparks can request an outfit change or object interaction but cannot bypass policy or force a denied action. A wardrobe request in a private conversation does not mean a live renderer changed; say proposed/unsupported when appropriate.
- Third-party asset imports are untrusted: validate format/size/texture dimensions, sandbox parsers, strip scripts/executables/network hooks, record licenses/consent, detect corrupted/missing assets, use safe fallback clothes/mesh and respect asset removal and version rollback. No facial/voice resemblance to a real person without authorization. Avoid distributing private models and meshes accidentally.
- User controls: visibility/privacy, clothed-default lock, outfit preset, reduced motion, audio/animation enable, object access, stop/reset/undo, notifications, hide avatar and delete exportable personal scene state. Child/age-unknown contexts never enable adult-restricted presentation or intimate interactions. Naked base asset is not proof of sexual consent or permission to publish it.

## F. Implementation stages and acceptance tests

| Stage | Work product | Evidence gate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A0: inspect/design | Pin source SHA; catalog canonical fields, decisions and licenses; choose candidates for 2D/3D and runtime through resource experiments; rig/region/wardrobe/object schema; accessible privacy modes | Reviewable asset mapping and design, no canonical mutation or invented anatomy |
| A1: adult base | Editable complete unclothed base with approved proportions, hair/eyes/ears/tail, rig, neutral pose, stable body-region mapping and clothing anchors; private authoring preview | Inspect geometry/measurements, coverage report and rig tests; default covered and restricted preview denied in unknown audience |
| A2: wardrobe core | Functional underwear/base layers through fully reconstructed canonical engineer outfit with tail/ear openings, socks, footwear, jacket and optional coat; inventory, layering, dressing/undressing checks | Run movement/occlusion, outfit restore/undo, absent/mismatched asset, coverage/privacy and clipping tests |
| A3: expressions/render | Coordinated face/ears/tail/hands/posture animation and isolated renderer bridged to existing INTERACT/UI; true result acknowledgment and headless fallback | Live actual renderer clicks and animation acknowledged; no double logging, false sensations or desktop click-through; foreground latency measured |
| A4: objects and environment | Versioned scene and prop inventory; pickup, inspect, place, handoff, offer/accept/decline, equip and state preservation for both actors | Two-user-turn sequence with real renderer and replay/restart/conflict/cancel tests; no fictitious physical control |
| A5: expansion/polish | More optional clothing categories, props, presets, contextual self-gestures, accessibility, import/export and multi-client scope | Permissioned multi-user privacy, renderer failure recovery, low-resource/gaming acceptance and supervised live quality review |

**Prerequisites/order:** A0 design can progress in parallel without delaying existing CORE → INTERACT (text/headless) → MEM commitments. A1/A2 can be produced offline before a renderer; A3 cannot be declared accepted before INTERACT's event/region/policy engine and actual UI renderer exist. A4 requires a coherent A1 rig, accepted interaction semantics, permissioned scene and real UI acknowledgment. Rich multi-user/shared scenes require SOCIAL/SAFE. Discord D0–D4 and RUN's 24/7 acceptance remain prerequisites for any **general internet/search** phase; PKG-AVATAR adds no search or network authority. Do not block Discord or always-on service waiting for a fully polished wardrobe or prop catalogue. Avatar should remain a replaceable client representation, not a new identity or service dependency.

**Minimum end-to-end demo:** with a fully clothed Sofía on screen, Sparks selects a notebook; the typed event authenticates Sparks and checks scene rights; renderer acknowledges pickup; Sparks offers it; Sofía may accept, decline or ask why; if accepted, the shared scene records the notebook's new holder and she may point to it while discussing a problem; restart recovers last acknowledged holder; a third user cannot see private scene contents; a private-mode toggle in a public session is denied; turning off the renderer leaves text-only INTERACT and conversation intact. No completed handoff may be claimed on a failed render.

**Verification:** asset integrity + canonical/provenance audit, focused unit/integration/negative tests, complete coordinated full pytest after implementation, supervised real rendered input/animation and recovery, audience/privacy review, first-token/total response latency and VRAM usage on actual chosen hardware, visual/art review. Record `passed`, `failed`, `not run`, or justified `not applicable` for each real and synthetic case. At the current documentation-only revision, all live mesh/render/wardrobe/prop acceptance = **not run**.
